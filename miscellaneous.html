TrackDrone greenhouse research platform

TrackDrone

I worked alongside an environmental studies professor and an electrical engineering student to conduct customer discovery and develop a greenhouse observation system. The platform carried a specialized camera provided to the research lab and was designed to travel autonomously along a rail spanning the greenhouse.

I focused on the finite-state machine and the system's main autonomous logic, using an Arduino Uno and simple ultrasonic distance sensors to control its movement. In the image, the custom rail mount had not yet been finalized.

TrackDrone working demonstration

TrackDrone Demo

This simple demonstration shows TrackDrone beginning an autonomous run along the rail. The platform was designed to travel back and forth across the full length of the greenhouse while carrying the specialized research camera.

Wave Finder sand pattern

Sand Art: Wave Finder

Wave Finder was an interactive art piece created for a one-day gallery hosted by Fruitcake, a UC Santa Cruz art club. Inspired by guitar and visual art, it used a six-inch, 8-ohm speaker, a quarter-inch acrylic sheet, a mono guitar output jack, an aluminum plate, and finely sifted beach sand.

A guitar signal drove the speaker and vibrated the plate. At particular resonant frequencies, the sand gathered into visible geometric patterns, allowing visitors to explore the relationship between sound and physical form.

Wave Finder in operation

Sand Art Demo

In this demonstration, a guitarist reaches one of the plate's resonant frequencies and a simple pattern emerges as the vibrating sand moves into shape.

Mellotron mechanism

Mellotron

I was asked to help implement a state machine for controlling a custom Mellotron project, contributing my experience with embedded control logic and system behavior.

Over the course of one day, we developed a working control system capable of coordinating the instrument's many complex mechanical movements.
